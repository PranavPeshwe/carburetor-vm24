What the choke actually changes

The lever opens a separate starter circuit

The VM24 choke uses a starter plunger, not a butterfly that blocks the main air inlet. The lever raises this plunger. [re]

Fuel enters the starter jet. Starter air helps form droplets. The open plunger admits this mixture to the engine side. [mikuni]

An open valve still needs a pressure difference

The starter uses intake depression. The VM manual specifies a closed throttle for starter operation. An open throttle weakens this signal. [mikuni]

The Engine draw control represents this driving condition. It is not an RPM or load measurement.

Droplets are not vapor

01 / LIQUID

Fuel reaches a metering jet

Amber markers represent liquid fuel. The jets restrict the supply before the fuel meets air.

02 / ATOMIZATION

Air breaks liquid into droplets

Smaller amber markers represent droplets. These droplets remain liquid. More air motion increases the model's breakup.

03 / EVAPORATION

Liquid changes into gas

Violet markers symbolize fuel vapor, which is invisible. Warm conditions increase this model's evaporation.

Cold conditions leave less fuel in vapor form. Starter enrichment supplies extra fuel. It does not heat the fuel or guarantee ignition.

Compare one cause at a time

Press Play. Then toggle Choke to animate the lever, plunger, and fuel path.

Select Cold start. Then switch Choke off without changing the other controls. Compare the starter flow and the relative fuel/air index.

Choke off closes only the starter path. The pilot circuit can still supply fuel. Its droplets remain visible in the close-up.

Then select Open throttle. The starter signal decreases even though the plunger remains open.

Select Engine off. The plunger remains open, but intake-driven flow stops. This separates valve position from the force that drives flow.

A consistent model, not a tuning prescription

All particles, plunger motion, and readouts share one model. This is an example of ★ referential transparency ★ in the model functions.

Droplet size, color, speed, circuit geometry, evaporation, and mixture indices are illustrative. The page does not predict AFR, RPM, power, ignition, or real jet settings.

The needle and jet controls express relative changes. They do not specify clip grooves or factory jet numbers.

An index of 1× represents the model's nominal fuel per air. This index is not a measured air-fuel ratio.

The engine creates the pressure difference

Air moves first

The engine lowers pressure downstream. Air enters the bore. The pressure difference draws fuel through the jets. [mikuni]

The needle changes the opening

The cable lifts the round slide and tapered needle together. A thinner needle section exposes a larger annular opening in the needle jet. [re]

Three operating regions, not three switches

The ranges below indicate approximate influence. They do not predict fuel percentages. The needle jet and main jet share one fuel path. [hm]

01 / Small opening

Pilot circuit

The pilot jet meters fuel near idle. The air screw adjusts pilot air. Its function differs from the throttle stop. [re]

02 / Part throttle

Needle metering

Fuel passes through the main jet before the needle jet. Needle clearance controls part-throttle metering. [re]

03 / Large opening

Main-jet metering

The main jet chiefly controls fuel near full throttle. The needle remains in the assembly, but its restriction matters less. [re]

The float regulates a reserve

A mechanical feedback loop

Fuel raises the float. The float closes the inlet needle. Consumption lowers the level and reopens the inlet. [mikuni]

This is ★ negative feedback ★. The float controls the reserve, while the jets meter the engine supply.

Try a fuel interruption

At half throttle, switch the fuel supply off. The model consumes the bowl reserve before the fuel traces disappear.

Then restore the supply. The inlet opens. The bowl refills. The float rises. Time and level are illustrative, not measured.

Cold start adds a separate passage

An enrichener, not an air flap

The VM24 lever lifts a starter plunger. This action admits extra fuel through the starter system. [re]

Compare closed and open throttle

Select Cold start. Then increase the throttle. The model reduces starter influence as the downstream pressure signal weakens. [mikuni]

What this model does—and does not claim

The same pure model drives the throttle, needle, float, circuit indicators, and captions. The shared model avoids conflicting visual states.

Geometry, circuit weights, float travel, inlet response, tracer speed, and bowl depletion time are educational choices. The model has no calibrated pressure, RPM, AFR, or fuel-flow output.

Throttle position is not engine speed. Engine speed and load also affect the pressure signal. The Engine draw control represents a qualitative driving condition, not measured speed or load. [mikuni]

The bench mount is an exhibit fixture. The section omits the near casting wall, air filter, manifold, hoses, and internal drillings outside the explanatory plane.

The approximate bands follow the Mikcarb guide and Mikuni explanations. They do not imply that the pilot abruptly stops at a marked boundary. [hm] [mikuni]
